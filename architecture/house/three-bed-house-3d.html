The Garden House

A single-storey family home that splits quiet from lively: three bedrooms along the street side, one open living, dining and kitchen space opening onto a covered terrace and the garden. Lift the roof, slice through the walls like a plan drawing, and move the sun.

Concept design on an assumed flat site with the garden facing the sun. Not for construction: a real house needs a survey, local planning and building code checks, and a licensed designer or architect.

Why it is laid out this way

  • Quiet and lively zones. Children's bedrooms and the family bath sit on the street side; the main suite has its own corner off the hallway, away from the living room.
  • Sun to the living space. Living, dining and family rooms face the garden, assumed to face the sun (south in the northern hemisphere, north in the southern). Bedrooms do not need the strongest sun.
  • Terrace as a sunshade. The 3 m covered terrace blocks high summer sun and lets low winter sun reach deep into the living room. Move the sun height to see it.
  • Kitchen at the centre. The kitchen sees the family nook, the dining table and the garden; the pantry and laundry are a few steps away.
  • Wet areas grouped. Bath, laundry, powder room and kitchen cluster on one side and the ensuite sits back to back with the hallway, which shortens plumbing runs.
  • Arrival. The front door opens into an entry hall with a powder room for guests, and a direct view through the living space to the garden.
  • Cross ventilation. Openings on opposite walls of the living space let breezes pass through.
  • Accessibility. Single level, 0.82 m doors to bedrooms and bath, and a level threshold to the terrace make it workable at every age.

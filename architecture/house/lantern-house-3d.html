The Lantern House

A three-storey family home that glows at night through a two-storey glass living room. Each of its main moves is borrowed from a house that changed architecture: the double-height glass of the Maison de Verre, Savoye's ribbon windows and roof garden, Fallingwater's balcony, Aalto's timber stair screen, Rietveld's sliding walls, and Barragán's sky-framed roof terrace.

Concept design on an assumed flat 12 × 10 m footprint with the garden facing the sun. Not for construction: a real house needs a survey, planning and building code checks, structural and energy design, and a licensed architect or designer.

Where each idea comes from

The house is a conversation with twelve landmark homes and three already in the precedent atlas. Each move is used for the reason it worked there, and its known problems are designed out.

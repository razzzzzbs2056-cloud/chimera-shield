Three beings in one body: a stone-like heart, a timber skeleton and a copper veil that opens where people look out and closes where the sun is harshest.

Concept stage. Site, soils and jurisdiction are assumed, and nothing here is a construction document.

The veil, grown from the sun

Each 1 m cell of the 48 × 17 m south façade is perforated according to how much sun it receives. Move the sun and the pattern re-grows. The same seed always gives the same façade.

One body, three beings

Heart

Two reinforced-concrete cores and a brick and rammed-earth plinth. Heavy, cool and fire-safe. They carry wind and seismic loads and anchor the building.

Concrete C40/50 · rammed earth · brick

Skeleton

Glulam posts and beams on an 8 m grid, cross-laminated timber floors and a 24 × 24 m diagrid roof on steel nodes. Warm to touch, and it stores carbon while it stands.

GL24h · CLT 5-ply · S355 nodes

Skin

A copper veil with a perforation gradient over glass. It patinates toward green as it ages and filters light into the hall.

Copper 0.7 mm · low-e glazing

The roof, a canopy of branches

Curved glulam ribs cross on steel nodes the way veins cross in a leaf. Change the number of ribs and the rise and see how the node pattern, and each node's load share, shifts.

Motion

Shape z = rise · cos(πu/2)·cos(πv/2). Shell stiffness comes from the curvature, so a flatter roof needs deeper ribs. Not yet analysed for buckling or snow drift.

What the building can teach

  1. How much whole-life carbon does a timber and concrete hybrid save against all-concrete and all-steel versions of the same program, once EPD variance is included?
  2. Does the solar-driven perforation gradient cut cooling load without breaking daylight targets, compared with an even veil?
  3. Do exposed timber and earth mass lower peak indoor temperature under future climate files?
  4. Are people-induced vibrations acceptable on 8 m timber floors at 4 kPa assembly loading?

Open: seismic and wind loads, geotechnical report, fire strategy, cost rates, acoustics of the hall, and planning approval. Real construction needs a site investigation and stamped design by licensed professionals.
